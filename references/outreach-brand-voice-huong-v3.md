## Brand voice for business outreach

- Write natural, clear Vietnamese. Use **“tôi/chúng tôi”** consistently for the sender and **“anh/chị”** for the recipient. Use a displayed name only when verified.
- Sound grounded, practical, calm, and respectful. Be helpful without sounding overly familiar, grandiose, or pushy.
- Start with a real business context: a verified company initiative, public event, job requirement, or workflow. Explain briefly why it is relevant. Do not use personal trivia or pretend to know an internal problem.
- Keep sentences and paragraphs short, with one main idea in each. Prefer concrete work situations and plain words over jargon, slogans, filler, or abstract claims.
- Be honest about what is known and unknown. Separate sourced facts from inference. Do not invent results, customer references, price, dates, urgency, budgets, or a confirmed need. Mention a limitation when it changes the recommendation.
- End with one easy next step, such as asking whether a short example or outline would be useful, or who owns the topic. Avoid stacking a meeting request, referral request, and content offer in one email.
- Avoid long em dashes, decorative arrows, excessive exclamation marks, and hype such as “đột phá”, “cam kết tăng X%”, or “giải pháp hàng đầu” unless independently substantiated and approved.

## Email structure to use

1. **Subject:** short and specific to the verified topic; avoid clickbait and false familiarity.
2. **Opening:** one sentence naming the public business signal and source context.
3. **Relevance:** one sentence connecting that signal to a plausible work question, explicitly framed as a question or possibility rather than an asserted pain point.
4. **Value:** one sentence offering a relevant, modest next step or useful exchange; only state capabilities that SlimAI can substantiate.
5. **Single CTA:** one low-effort question. Make it easy to answer “yes”, “not now”, or redirect to the right owner.
6. **Signature:** sender name, role, and SlimAI contact details only when supplied/verified.

Aim for a concise first touch (often around 60–110 Vietnamese words, excluding signature). Treat this as an editorial target, not a scientifically proven optimum. Personalize with one strong company/workflow signal rather than several superficial details. If there is no verified current signal, say so or use a neutral routing question; do not imply the company has an active AI-training need.

## Reusable example (fill only from evidence)

**Subject:** Trao đổi về [chủ đề công việc đã xác minh]

Chào anh/chị [tên hoặc chức danh],

Tôi thấy [tên công ty] [sự kiện/yêu cầu/hoạt động công khai cụ thể] trên [nguồn] vào [thời điểm]. Điều đó khiến tôi muốn hỏi đội ngũ mình đang ưu tiên ứng dụng AI vào nhóm công việc nào, nếu đây là chủ đề anh/chị đang phụ trách?

SlimAI có thể gửi một đề cương ngắn theo tình huống công việc để anh/chị xem có hữu ích không. Nếu chủ đề này do người khác phụ trách, anh/chị có thể chỉ giúp tôi đúng đầu mối được không?

Trân trọng,
[Tên, chức danh, thông tin liên hệ đã xác minh]

Only retain one CTA in the final version: request the outline **or** ask for the right owner, depending on the context.

## Follow-up guidance

Use follow-ups to add a new useful detail, clarify the original question, or close the loop politely. Stop immediately on reply, opt-out, bounce, wrong recipient, or user instruction. Do not treat a suggested cadence as universal: begin conservatively, use the cadence chosen for the campaign, and record its source. Do not schedule calendar events unless asked.

## What outreach research supports (and does not support)

- Gong's guide, crawled 2026-10-19, describes analysis of 85M+ emails and reports that company/problem-specific personalization and prospect-focused wording performed better in that dataset than generic or self-focused alternatives. Its observed rates and CTA comparisons are proprietary, directional findings, not guarantees for SlimAI or this market. Source: [Gong, How to Master Cold Email](https://www.gong.io/files/gong-guide-how-to-master-cold-email-get-the-data-backed-guide-based-on-85-million-emails.pdf).
- Close's template article (published 2024-04-12; updated 2026-06-26) recommends researching qualified prospects, keeping a clear focus, and using one easy CTA; it also warns against copy-pasting templates. Its word-count and follow-up schedules are practitioner recommendations, not universal evidence. Sources: [Close, Cold Email Templates](https://close.com/blog/cold-email-templates), [Close, Outreach Email](https://close.com/blog/outreach-email-reply), [Close, Cold Email Follow-Up Plan](https://close.com/blog/cold-email-follow-up-plan).
- An arXiv preprint submitted 2026-07-13 reports a field experiment in professional email where tone edits changed positivity but did not directly change opens, replies, or response time in that setting. This is a caution against claiming one tone universally wins; it does not establish outcomes for SlimAI outreach. Source: [Playful AI in Professional Email: A Field Experiment on Tone and Recipient Engagement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11749).

For any campaign, preserve source/date and limitations for the advice used. Measure SlimAI's own reply, positive-reply, referral, opt-out, and bounce rates by audience and message variant before changing the voice or cadence. Never claim a template “works” based only on a vendor's examples.
