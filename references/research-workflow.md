# Research workflow

Use this workflow for new campaigns and refreshes. It is designed to keep account evidence, person identity, buying relevance, and contact routes distinct. Do not move a record to the final Sheet until it passes the relevant checks below.

## 1. Establish the campaign and baseline

Confirm from the user's request and explicitly selected materials:

- Offer, seller, verified value proposition, sender identity, and relevant exclusions.
- Target geography, industries or account types, buyer functions, requested output, and target number of people.
- Which existing list or Sheet belongs to this campaign.

Ask only about gaps that could materially change account selection or the buyer map. If a detail is not critical, record it as an assumption and continue. Do not borrow another campaign's product, criteria, lead records, or live Sheet.

Before searching, read the selected account/contact list and relevant Sheet tabs. Build a deduplication baseline using account domain/legal entity and, for people, profile URL plus employer. Note the existing qualified-person count, missing coverage, and stale records. A refresh adds or changes records; it does not silently replace the campaign history.

## 2. Turn the offer into a search brief

Write a short internal brief with:

1. The problem and workflows the offer addresses.
2. Account inclusion and exclusion rules.
3. Buyer map: budget owner, functional owner, internal champion, technical evaluator, and end-user/business-unit owner where applicable.
4. Title and keyword variants in the relevant languages.
5. What public evidence would count as a current need signal, a weaker proxy, or no signal.
6. A target number of distinct qualified people and a larger working pool to account for exclusions and duplicates.

Keep confirmed user criteria separate from research hypotheses. Do not use contact volume as a substitute for account fit. A recruiter may be included as a routing contact when relevant, but label that role clearly and do not score the person as a buyer without evidence.

## 3. Build and verify an account pool

Search more than one source family. Match the source mix to the market; likely sources include official company sites and reports, company news, public tenders/events, credible industry news, public LinkedIn company pages/posts, public Facebook business pages, job listings, event and association pages, and professional directories. Search the company name, domain, local-language variants, subsidiaries, brands, and office location. Treat each source as a discovery path, then verify material claims against the company or original publisher where possible.

For each candidate account:

- Confirm the legal/trading identity, domain, target geography, and relevant business unit/location.
- Capture only evidence relevant to the offer: workflows, likely user groups, scale, buying structure, and need/timing signals.
- Record the source URL, publisher/source type, publication or event date when available, and research check date.
- Separate direct evidence from inference. Treat headcount as a dated estimate/range unless an authoritative source gives an exact figure.
- Check parent/subsidiary and shared procurement relationships before counting separate accounts.

Do a quick fit screen before deep person research. Remove clear mismatches early. Keep uncertain accounts in a research-needed group instead of presenting them as qualified.

## 4. Discover people with a role map

For each selected account, search the official company page and public professional sources using the role map from step 2. Search multiple title variants and seniority levels; do not assume one department owns the purchase.

Use a deliberate priority order:

1. Functional owner closest to the offer's use case.
2. L&D/HR/People or another program owner when that function is relevant.
3. Budget owner or senior sponsor, when public evidence identifies one.
4. Technical or business-unit evaluator where needed.
5. Recruiter or hiring contact as a route to the right owner, when direct owners are not discoverable or a relevant hiring signal is public.

Search in repeated waves rather than stopping at the first profile:

1. **LinkedIn-first discovery:** search exact company and location with title/function variants; search public LinkedIn company pages, employee lists visible to the researcher, profiles, posts, and relevant public job/event content. Use search-engine indexing for exact profiles when platform search is limited, then open the canonical profile in the browser when accessible. Search both Vietnamese and English title variants and adjacent functions from the buying map.
2. **Company-site discovery:** inspect leadership/team, news, academy/training, press releases, event speakers, annual/sustainability reports, procurement, contact, and newsroom pages. Search the company domain for the person's name and role.
3. **Public social and event discovery:** inspect official Facebook business pages and public posts, public webinar/conference speaker lists, association pages, partner announcements, podcasts, and public professional communities. Use only pages/posts accessible without bypassing a login, privacy setting, or other access control.
4. **Role-signal discovery:** use current job listings, public hiring posts, tenders, training announcements, and org-change news to identify the likely buyer function or routing contact. A hiring post is evidence about hiring, not automatically a sales contact route or proof of course demand.

Search queries should combine exact company name, location, function/title variants, and terms from public hiring or initiative signals. Prefer exact public LinkedIn profiles, but corroborate name, employer, title, and location with a second public source when available. A search snippet alone is a discovery clue, not enough to claim current employment if the profile or source is ambiguous. When a browser is available and the user has authorized/connected access, use it to inspect public profile and page context that search snippets omit; do not automate bulk profile scraping or evade platform restrictions.

Record name exactly as displayed, title as stated by the source, employer/location match, profile URL, evidence date, source URL, role in the buying process, and confidence. Preserve the exact verified LinkedIn slug; never construct a profile URL from a name. If LinkedIn blocks access, record that it was not reachable and rely only on other accessible public evidence. Do not bypass sign-in or access controls.

Find more than one person at an account when they represent distinct roles or useful routing paths. Do not add several recruiters from one account just to increase count. A recruiter contact is qualified only as a recruiter/routing contact unless evidence supports more.

## 5. Verify contact channels person by person

Run a separate, channel-by-channel route search for each surviving person; do not assume a route found for one employee belongs to another. First establish the exact person, current company, role, and canonical profile. Then search each channel independently so a missing email does not end the search. Use the following source families when available and relevant:

- Official company site: team/leadership pages, newsroom, event pages, downloadable reports, staff biographies, contact pages, and published documents.
- LinkedIn: exact profile contact details visible publicly, profile headline/about/activity, company posts that name the person, and public posts where the person explicitly publishes a professional route. Keep profile URL and route-evidence URL separate.
- Facebook: official company pages and public posts, public event/group posts, and named professional posts. Do not treat a personal Facebook profile as a business route unless the person explicitly presents that route for work inquiries.
- Zalo: official company Zalo OA or a named professional Zalo explicitly published by the person/company for work contact. Do not infer a Zalo account from a phone number or search for a private account.
- Events, associations, partner pages, webinars, podcasts, public tenders, and professional directories: use a listed route only when the source identifies the same person and its professional purpose.
- Public job listings: may identify the recruiter or HR routing contact; label the route `recruiting only` unless the same source explicitly invites other business inquiries. Never repurpose an application-only email/phone for sales.

For every person, run a compact query set per channel, adapted to local spelling and language. Examples: exact quoted name + company + `email` / `contact`; exact name + company + `điện thoại` / `mobile` / `phone`; exact name + company + `Zalo`; exact name + company + `LinkedIn` / `Facebook`; and a domain-restricted search of the official company site. Search a known profile URL/name together with the channel term to avoid same-name collisions. Open the original result where possible; a search snippet alone is a lead for verification, not proof. Do not repeat near-identical searches after the same source set is exhausted.

Search public professional sources for:

- A named work email or role-based company mailbox.
- A named direct work phone or business mobile.
- A phone/Zalo the person publicly publishes for professional contact, recruitment, or business inquiries.
- The exact LinkedIn profile.
- Another public professional route only when useful to the campaign.

For phone/email evidence, preserve context in the record. Distinguish a named direct work route, publicly posted recruiter route, generic company channel, switchboard, masked/partial detail, and unverified third-party claim. Keep company-wide channels on the Account record. A number appearing beside a job ad is a recruiter route only when the source actually associates it with that named contact; do not use recruitment-only routes to pitch a product. Never infer a person's identity from an email alias, area code, masked phone, or similar name. A public direct phone is eligible for a person-specific sales field only when the source ties it to the named person and clearly presents it as a professional/business contact route.

Use bounded query variants: exact name + company + channel term, exact profile URL/name + channel term, and company + role/hiring context. Check the source page itself when accessible. Stop after a reasonable set of distinct query variants has been checked; record “not found publicly” instead of implying exhaustive certainty. Do not search for private, leaked, purchased, or access-controlled contact details.

Each route must have its own URL/source, context, check date, and verification status. Record the exact source text/context in a short evidence note, not just the search query. Do not guess email patterns, fill missing digits, use data brokers/leaks, or promote a generic route into a personal contact. A public professional route does not imply consent to send a message.

### Contact-rich mode: prioritize people with both email and phone

Automatically use this mode when the user asks for leads with email and phone, contact details, reachable contacts, or contact-ready leads. The goal is to maximize the number of **qualified people with both** a verified professional email and a verified direct work phone—not to manufacture a guarantee that every qualified lead will have both.

For each high-fit person, complete separate email and phone searches before moving on. Give extra attention to public sources that associate a named person with direct business routes: official leadership/team bios, speaker and event pages, association/committee pages, press releases, public professional profiles/posts, and the person's own business site. Search exact name + current company + local and English variants such as `email`, `contact`, `liên hệ`, `điện thoại`, `di động`, `mobile`, `phone`, and `hotline`; search the person's exact profile URL/name with each channel term and use domain-restricted searches for the official company site. Open the original page and verify the person-route association. If an authorized LinkedIn session is connected, use it to inspect that person's accessible professional profile and public contact details; do not bypass login/access controls, automate bulk scraping, or treat a profile that is merely visible in search as verified contact evidence.

Use distinct route labels in the People record:

- `direct work email` / `direct work phone`: the source names or clearly ties that professional route to the person.
- `company email` / `company switchboard or hotline`: a shared organizational route, stored as a fallback route and never copied into the person's direct-contact field.
- `not found publicly`, `not accessible`, `conflicting`, or `not searched`: include source/check context and reason as applicable.

Keep two separate measures: (1) contact-ready people with **both direct channels verified**, and (2) qualified people with one or more usable public professional routes, including clearly labeled company routes. Rank only among qualified people: honor any user-stated priority, then use fit, current need evidence, and confidence as the primary ordering; use completeness of direct routes as a tie-breaker or as an explicit user-requested prioritization. Do not replace a higher-fit buyer with a low-fit person solely to make the contact-ready total look larger. If the user specifies a minimum number with both channels, report the achieved count and shortfall plainly.

Every contact-rich result must include the person's verified LinkedIn/profile link when available, direct work email, direct work phone, each channel's status, and a source link for each populated direct route. Show company-level inbox/phone in separate fields. For missing direct channels, write `chưa tìm thấy công khai` (or the accurate inaccessible/conflicting status); never leave a blank that looks like an oversight. Report a compact coverage summary, for example: `qualified N người; M người có đủ email và điện thoại trực tiếp; K người có một kênh trực tiếp; P người chỉ có đầu mối công ty`. State the search families checked and any remaining gaps. Only use email drafts for a person's verified work email under the existing outreach and Gmail rules; a company inbox does not qualify as that person's address.

### Contact coverage checklist

For every qualified person, record one status per requested channel: `verified`, `not found publicly after checks`, `not accessible`, `conflicting`, or `not searched` (with reason). At minimum, assess work email, work phone/business mobile, LinkedIn, and professional Zalo when relevant; include Facebook or other professional routes when the campaign requests broad social research. “Not found” means the listed public searches were checked, not that no such contact exists. Do not leave an empty cell that could be mistaken for an unsearched channel. If a company has a switchboard or shared inbox, store it separately as a company route; never relabel it as the lead's direct contact.

## 6. Qualify, deduplicate, and score

Apply account qualification and contact qualification separately. A strong account does not make an unrelated employee a good lead; a well-connected recruiter does not prove account buying intent.

Before scoring:

- Deduplicate accounts by legal entity/domain and buying-unit independence.
- Deduplicate people by exact profile URL, then compare name, employer, and role; do not merge people solely because names match.
- Recheck that the evidence supports the stated account, person, title, geography, and route.
- Classify signal recency and strength. Prior activity can justify requalification, but does not prove current need or budget.
- Mark conflicts, missing fields, stale evidence, and inferences explicitly.

Score only from evidence that has been captured. Keep account fit, person fit, confidence, and stage as separate fields. Explain material points, use the active campaign's rubric, and do not treat a fit score as purchase probability.

## 7. Run a coverage loop before stopping

Compare the qualified unique-person count with the requested target. Also check coverage by account and buying role so the result is not padded with duplicate recruiters or multiple people at one company when broader coverage is needed.

Before stopping—even when the people target has been met—check channel coverage for each priority lead. A person count does not prove that contact research is complete. If direct contact coverage remains weak, continue the channel sweep for A-priority leads and other high-fit accounts, while preserving the same verification bar.

In contact-rich mode, also compare the number of qualified people with both direct routes against the user's requested contact-ready target, if any. If it is below target, continue targeted person-by-person searches and expand to other qualified accounts with a better chance of named public contacts. Keep fit and contact completeness separate, and report the final counts even when the target is unmet.

If below target, continue in this order:

1. Search additional role/title synonyms at already qualified accounts.
2. Search public company hiring/contact posts for named recruiting or HR routing contacts.
3. Expand to the next accounts that meet the same campaign criteria.
4. Re-run deduplication and verification on new candidates.

Stop when the target is met and high-priority channel coverage has been checked, or reasonable source/role/channel expansion is exhausted. Report the number requested, number qualified, unique-account coverage, which source families and role/channel variants were checked, routes verified by channel, and the specific reason for any shortfall. Never lower the qualification bar silently to meet a count or claim “all channels exhausted” without recording which were actually checked.

## 8. Write the research record and perform a final audit

Write only verified and appropriately qualified records to the selected campaign Sheet. Preserve its IDs and history. Keep company routes on Accounts; keep person-specific routes on People with their evidence and status. Mark missing channels explicitly. Include checked date and direct evidence links for material claims.

Before handing back, audit a sample and all high-priority rows for:

- Duplicate person/account records.
- Wrong-company or stale-role matches.
- Guessed, masked, misattributed, or generic routes in person-specific fields.
- Unsupported score points, need claims, or headcount claims.
- Broken/malformed links and missing source dates where dates are available.
- Requested volume versus actual qualified unique people.
- Per-person route status and evidence for every channel the user requested; ensure recruiting-only, generic, inaccessible, conflicting, and unverified routes are labeled correctly.

Summarize what was verified, what remains unknown, the coverage achieved, and any next research action. Do not describe research-only contacts as emailed, approved, or contacted.
